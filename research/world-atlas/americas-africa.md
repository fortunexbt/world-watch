# Americas and Africa: curated complementary-source candidates

Research cut prepared 21 October 2026. `americas-africa.jsonl` contains **174 discovered candidate records** across 15 publisher-country codes: NG 51, HT 16, GH 13, ZA 13, KE 11, BR 9, JM 8, CO 8, MX 8, BB 7, UG 7, BZ 6, SN 6, TT 6, BS 5. Counts describe rows in this contribution; they do not show national coverage completeness or source independence. Every row remains `discovered`; no feed, collection, reuse, redistribution, model-use, or ongoing access permission was established.

## Research approach

I prioritized locally based original reporting and first-party monitors that can expose public activity, rights harms, emergency conditions, infrastructure disruptions, displacement, or consequential local change. I included national and regional sources in different languages and organization types. The entries use the publisher's country code; known cross-border reporting is called out in `notes` (for example Mail & Guardian, MFWA, Article 19, CIVICUS, and NAN). Related outlets from one corporate newsroom or member network are marked as shared lineage where known.

Source links were taken from publisher pages, institutional publisher catalogs, or official organization/service pages. Examples include the [National Institute for Nigerian Languages newspaper catalog](https://www.ninlan.edu.ng/newspapers/), [National Library of Nigeria locator](https://newspaperlocator.nln.gov.ng/search), [Media Council of Kenya report (Kenyan media context)](https://mediacouncil.or.ke/sites/default/files/downloads/State%20of%20the%20Media%20Report%202024.pdf), [South African Press Council member pages and publisher disclosures](https://www.presscouncil.org.za/), [Media Foundation for West Africa](https://mfwa.org/), and source-specific institutional or publisher provenance links in each JSONL record. Not every candidate has had its endpoint, update cadence, terms, or technical access independently tested.

These are useful seeds, not 50-source country packs. The Caribbean additions improve island-state coverage but remain deliberately short: the small national media markets have limited independent publisher lineages, and a row count must not be mistaken for 50 distinct sources. Trinidad and Tobago Newsday was excluded after its January 2026 closure was reported by [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/3077797ddb49afed0dc0e81e762e0083). Senegal and Uganda add distinct Francophone West and East African local reporting, rights/media monitors and first-party public instruments, but their records also remain candidates pending access and lineage review. Sparse countries still need more local-language reporting and locally operated instruments. Do not fill the remaining numbers with stale newspaper titles, multiple sections from one publisher, duplicated agency rewrites, or global sources assigned the wrong publisher country. Where small states lack many domestic publishers, add a separately labeled cross-border source only after its home country and coverage are verified.

## Important limits before any integration

- A publisher page, public report, or visible alert does not grant article storage, full-text reuse, model processing, derivative display, or redistribution. Most rows explicitly leave these unknown. The Agência Brasil page states a specific journalism-republication rule; follow its linked policy and still check partner-material notices.
- The Nigeria newspaper catalog and university list help establish publisher title/domain, but not current usefulness, access, independence, or rights. The additional publisher directory links to official domains but is not a quality endorsement. The National Library locator is a discovery tool, not an event feed.
- Several civic monitors publish periodic, election-only, or case-based reports. They should be used for contextual evidence or material reports, not treated as continuously refreshed incident feeds.
- Official police and government statements represent the issuing authority's account. Human-rights reports represent their documented methods and sources. Preserve attribution and seek independent corroboration.
- Haiti’s UHM, BME and PNH endpoints, some country newsroom paths, and several API/feed assumptions remain unverified; their rows are research leads and must not be presented as working integrations.
- The directories and records do not imply complete visibility. Reporting and observatory silence is not evidence that no event occurred.
